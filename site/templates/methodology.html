Standard

The WHO 2017 joint statement asks for summary results on the registry within 12 months of primary completion (WHO). This is an ethical norm, not a legal duty. The legal-duty axis below is separate and never changes a WHO category.

Rules: $method

Each trial is classified as of the data date. The clock is the primary completion date; a date given only as a month or a year sits on the last day of that period. One threshold, 395 days (12 months plus 30 days for quality control), is used for both cuts.

  1. No reporting requirement: status WITHDRAWN, or TERMINATED with an actual enrolment of 0.
  2. Status NOT_YET_RECRUITING, RECRUITING, ENROLLING_BY_INVITATION, ACTIVE_NOT_RECRUITING or SUSPENDED (a pause, not an exemption): ongoing until 395 days after the primary completion date, then status overdue. An open trial without a primary completion date is ongoing.
  3. Status COMPLETED or TERMINATED with an actual primary completion date:
    • Completed, not yet due: no more than 395 days since primary completion.
    • Due, no results submitted: otherwise, if no results have been submitted.
    • Due, submitted but returned in quality control: results submitted, none posted, and the latest registry event on the submission is a reset.
    • Due, results submitted in time or late: results first submitted no more than, or more than, 395 days after primary completion.
  4. Inconsistent record: a finished trial whose primary completion date is missing or only estimated, and every other status (such as UNKNOWN).

The submission date counts, not the date results were posted. Status overdue and inconsistent records are never counted as unreported.

Comparison: $comparison

The comparison rules reimplement the code behind Keestra et al., Trials 22:385 (2021) (LeeSean96/GlobalHealthRanking, MIT, Copyright 2020 Yi Nian Lee), so the $method corrections can be measured against them. Each trial is classified as of the data date, in this order:

  1. No reporting requirement: status WITHDRAWN or SUSPENDED.
  2. Status COMPLETED or TERMINATED:
    • Completed, not yet due: primary completion date fewer than 395 days (365 days plus 30 days for quality control) before the data date.
    • Due, results submitted: otherwise, if a results first-submitted date exists.
    • Due, no results submitted: otherwise, including a trial with no primary completion date at all.
  3. Ongoing: status NOT_YET_RECRUITING, RECRUITING, ENROLLING_BY_INVITATION or ACTIVE_NOT_RECRUITING with a primary completion date fewer than 30 days before the data date (or in the future).
  4. Inconsistent or stale record: everything else, such as status UNKNOWN or an open status well past its primary completion date.

As in the original code, a primary completion date given only as a month is read as the first of that month, whether the date is actual or estimated is not read, and the submission date counts, not the date results were posted.

Legal duty: United States (FDAAA 801)

This tracker measures the WHO standard. Readers who need legal compliance should start from the trackers built for it: the FDAAA TrialsTracker (US law) and the EU TrialsTracker (EU rules), both from the Bennett Institute.

ClinicalTrials.gov does not say which trials are applicable clinical trials (ACTs) under 42 CFR Part 11, so we infer a probable ACT when every criterion of 11.10 and 11.22(b) that the registry shows holds: interventional study; an FDA-regulated drug other than phase 1, or an FDA-regulated device that is not a feasibility study; a site in the United States or its territories, or a product exported from there; not withdrawn. Trials run only under an IND or IDE number cannot be seen, because those numbers are not public, so a trial without a US site or export reads not determined, as does any trial missing one of these fields.

  1. A probable ACT with a primary completion date before 18 Jan 2017 is not determined: Part 11 results duties then cover only approved products (11.42), which the registry does not show.
  2. Results submitted: results submitted, whatever the date (11.44 deadlines are about submission).
  3. No more than 395 days (one year, 11.44(a), plus 30 days for quality control) after primary completion: not yet due. After that, an estimated primary completion date is not determined.
  4. A certification of delay or an extension request on the record (dispFirstSubmitDate): not yet due until two years after it was submitted, the 11.44(b) and (c) backstop.
  5. Otherwise: appears overdue under FDAAA, absent an unpublished extension.

These rules re-check the FDAAA TrialsTracker's (ebmdatalab/clinicaltrials-act-tracker, MIT) against the regulation's text, and follow the text where the two differ. Only FDA finds noncompliance: we quote ClinicalTrials.gov's "fdaaa801Violation" flag, set on information from FDA, and add nothing to it. For the UK and the EU the legal duty is not determined; see the overview.

Modifications to the ClinicalTrials.gov data

The data were requested from the ClinicalTrials.gov API $api_version, interventional studies only, with only the fields needed for classification; no investigator, official or contact fields are fetched. No record is edited. The changes made are: dates given as a month or a year are stored as the last day of that period (then read as above); each trial is assigned one category by the rules above (and one by methodology v1.0, in the downloads); and only counts per category, plus the per-trial and per-sponsor downloads linked from the overview, are published.
